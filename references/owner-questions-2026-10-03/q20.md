# Q20: a crossed piece in the exact (nearest) class

written 2026-10-03 (complete). read-only on the tracked tree; probes in `probes-q20/` (`probe1.py`, `probe2.py`,
`probe3.py`, run with `PYTHONPATH=<repo>` and `envs/intervals` python). "probe N" marks a measured fact; the rest is read
from the sources cited.

## 1. the question and what is built today

**the question** (`HANDOFF.md` "open questions for the owner", Q20 bullet): to nearest, an exact end beside a float
end can come out past it. `rootn((10 ** -30, 1.0000000000000003e-30], 5)`: the exact end gives `1/10 ** 6` exactly
(open, as the operand's end is open), the float end's true image is about `1/10 ** 6 + 5.2e-23` and rounds to nearest
onto the double `1e-06` = `1/10 ** 6 - 4.5e-23`, which is BELOW the exact end. before `912558b` the reversed pair reached
`kernel.piece` and raised `ValueError` (the fuzz on CI found it, run 37098878528; plan §2 "fuzz-rootn-crossed").

**what is built** (`912558b`, verified by reading the diff): `intervals/functions.py::_settled` gained
`if lo > hi: return hi, hi_closed, lo, lo_closed`, and `intervals/reverse.py::branch_preimage` the same `elif a > b`
swap. the result is **the piece between the two values, each value keeping its own flag**: `[1e-06, 1/1000000)`,
and `pown_rev` the same (probe 1, 2026-10-03). the outward class is unchanged: `(1/1000000, 1.0000000000000002e-06)`
(probe 1). pins: `tests/test_functions.py::test_rootn_ends_crossed_by_rounding`,
`tests/test_reverse.py::test_pown_rev_ends_crossed_by_rounding`, and `@example(rng=random.Random(94807))` on
`tests/test_extreme_floats_functions.py::test_every_value_is_in_the_result`.

**why only to nearest, only mixed pieces** (inferred from `functions._Function.end` and `reverse._end`, confirmed by
probe 3): each end is typed on its own (`is_float(x)`); an exact end never rounds, a float end rounds to nearest in
`MultiInterval` and in the `want` direction in `OutwardMultiInterval`. directed rounding moves an end away from the
piece, so it can never pass an exact end on the far side (`DOWN(f(x_lo)) <= f(x_lo) <= f(x_hi)`); nearest can, by up to
half an ulp. two float ends cannot cross either: round-to-nearest is monotone, they can only squeeze onto one double
(probe 3: `[1e-30, 1.0000000000000003e-30].rootn(5)` is `[1e-06]`). so the crossing needs a mixed piece whose two
images lie within half an ulp of the result: ends within about half an ulp of each other, or closer after a
compressing function (`rootn` divides the relative gap by n; `log` compresses more). the plan's probe at 15 exact
points with float partners one to five doubles away found only `rootn` and `pown_rev` (plan §2 "fuzz-rootn-crossed").

**a fact that decides a lot**: crossing implies both ends round to the SAME double. if `d = round(v)` is past the exact
end `e` (so `v > e > d` or the mirror), then `|v - d| <= ulp/2` puts `e` strictly between `d` and `v`, hence within
half an ulp of `d` on the near side, so `round(e) = d` too (probe 3: `round_rational(1/10 ** 6, NEAREST) == 1e-06`,
and the top of the image, `1/10 ** 6 + 5.2e-23`, is below `1e-06`'s upper midpoint at `1/10 ** 6 + 6.1e-23`). a
crossed piece is therefore a squeezed piece whose exact end refused to move. this is what makes the alternatives
comparable: every option is a choice about what to do with ONE double and ONE exact value a sliver apart.

## 2. what the class promises, and what the applicator does

**the nearest class's promise, as written**: `README.md` (rounding bullet, line 196): "`MultiInterval` rounds a float
result to nearest"; the divergence table row "rounding": "`MultiInterval` rounds to nearest as python's float". `v2-plan.md`
"current design" > arithmetic > flags at rounded ends: "to nearest, flags are conservative, not a promise (the suite
checks the nearest mode against the closure) ... an exact operand never loses its true value". `intervals/rounding.py`
line 64 and `intervals/modulo.py` line 33 say the same in code comments. **nowhere does the nearest class promise that
a result holds its true values** when a float is involved; that is the outward class's promise (README: "rounds it
outward to the tightest float enclosure of the exact result").

**the promise as tested** (`tests/test_extreme_floats_functions.py::test_every_value_is_in_the_result`, read):
for a sampled point x of the operand, outward must hold f(x); nearest must hold f(x) when the operand has exact
ends ("if exacts: `_holds(nearest, sign)`"), and for a float operand "the nearest double to it is in the closure of
a piece of the result" (`_nearest_holds`). so the tested contract for a mixed piece is BOTH: exact sample points
land inside, and the nearest double of every image value is in the closure. `[1e-06, 1/1000000)` satisfies both
(the image's exact samples are not in it, but no exact sample of `(10 ** -30, ...]` exists at the open end, and the
nearest double of every image value is `1e-06`, closed).

**the applicator's rule, which the fix copied** (`intervals/applicator.py::evaluate_box`, read): the low end is
`min(lows)` and the high end `max(highs)` over the corner values, each corner typed by its own operands
(`_rounds(args)`: a float among finite args), each flag by attainment at that corner. nothing orders the corners by
which operand end they came from. **the nearest class already produces the crossed shape from plain `*`** (probe 2,
2026-10-03): `MultiInterval(1 - 10 ** -30, 1.0) * Fraction(1, 3)` is `[0.3333333333333333, 1/3 - 10 ** -30 / 3]`: the
float corner (1.0, 1/3), whose python product `0.3333333333333333` is below the exact corner's value, became the LOW
end, and the exact corner the HIGH end, roles swapped against the operand's ends. with an open exact end
(`(1 - 10 ** -30, 1.0) * 1/3`) that piece would hold no true value either; the applicator never raised because it
never assumed an order. so `912558b` did not invent a semantics: it made `_Function.end`/`reverse._end`, which pick
their ends by the operand's ends, agree with the applicator, which picks by value. the outward class does the same by
value: `O(1 - 10 ** -30, 1.0) * 1/3` is `(0.3333333333333333, 0.33333333333333337)`, the exact corner's value swallowed
inside the float corner's enclosure (probe 2).

**the library's two typing conventions** (grep `has_finite_float`, `float_operands=`, 2026-10-03; see section 5):
per END for `+ - * /` (applicator corners), the elementary functions (`_Function.end`), the reverse ops (`reverse._end`);
per OPERAND ("any finite float end makes every number a float, rounded once") for `pow_` (the float rule is pow's, per
operand), `hypot` (`float_operands=`), `fma`, `mod`/`floordiv`, the step functions (`rounding.round_piece`) and the
numeric functions `mid`/`rad`/`wid`/`mag`/`mig`. probe 3: `M(1, 2.0) ** 2` is `[1, 4.0]` (pown, per end) but
`M(1, 2.0) ** M(2)` is `[1.0, 4.0]` (pow, per operand); `M(1, 2.0).sqrt()` is `[1, 1.4142135623730951]`, `.hypot(M(0))`
is `[1.0, 2.0]`. Q20's option C is the per-operand convention applied to the per-end sites.

**verdict on "is a piece with no true value acceptable for the nearest class"**: by the written and tested contract,
yes. the class never promised a true value at a float end; it promised python's nearest double, and `1e-06` is in the
closure. the exact end's contribution (`1/10**6`, excluded) is exactly right: the operand excluded `10**-30`. the
piece is a sliver `[d, e)` of width 4.5e-23 whose only point of interest is `d`; it is strange to look at, but the
applicator has handed out the same sliver from `*` since M12 without anyone noticing, which says how often it is seen.

## 4. the options

### A. built: the piece between the two values, each keeping its flag (`[1e-06, 1/1000000)`)

**meaning**: `_settled` and `branch_preimage` swap a reversed pair; the float end is python's nearest double with the
operand end's flag; the exact end is the exact value with the operand end's flag. the result is the hull of the two
ends' images as the applicator would compute it.

**pros**: (1) one rule for `+ - * /`, the functions and the reverse ops: "the ends are the least and greatest end values,
each typed and flagged by its source" (`evaluate_box`, `_settled`, `branch_preimage`). (2) keeps every attained exact
value (closed variant: `[1e-06, 1/1000000]` holds `1/10**6`), so `_holds(nearest)` for exact samples keeps passing and
"an exact operand never loses its true value" stays literally true for the exact end. (3) keeps the nearest double of
every image value in the closure (`_nearest_holds`). (4) the flags carry the operand's information unchanged: open means
"the limit, not attained". (5) smallest code; already pinned, fuzzed x10 green on CI.

**cons**: (1) the open-exact-end case yields a piece holding no true value; a reader who knows the operand may be
surprised that `1e-06` is in and `1/10**6 + 5e-23` is not (but that is the nearest class on every float end). (2) the
piece's orientation hides which end came from which operand end; `r.sqrt()` crosses back silently. (3) it is a rule
nobody will remember; it must be documented in `v2-plan.md` "flags at rounded ends" or it reads as a bug again.

**when A is the better choice**: when the priority is a single, type-faithful rule across all three end producers and
the distinguishing promise "int and Fraction exact, never rounded" (README table, D3) must hold end by end, even inside
a mixed piece. users: anyone who relies on exact ends surviving arithmetic (domain ends, `log` at an exact 0, solver
boxes with exact bounds) while tolerating python-float ends.

### B. both ends closed (`[1e-06, 1/1000000]`)

**meaning**: on a crossing, close both flags, as the squeeze-to-a-point rule does (`evaluate_box`: "a rounded flag is
conservative, so the piece keeps that point").

**pros**: (1) uses the existing licence "to nearest, flags are conservative, not a promise"; (2) the piece then holds
`1/10**6`, the limit of the image, so it is a closed enclosure of the image's closure around the double; the closed and
the open variant of the operand give the same result, which some find tidier; (3) tiles with a neighbour starting at
`1/10**6` either way.

**cons**: (1) it adds an exact point the operand explicitly excluded: an exact end's flag is the one bit of information
in the result the nearest class can get exactly right, and B throws it away for no soundness gain (`_nearest_holds`
already passed). (2) inconsistent with the applicator, whose flags come from attainment at the corners and would
leave `(1 - 10**-30, 1.0) * 1/3` half-open; B would have to change `evaluate_box` too to be one rule, or be a
functions-only special case. (3) it makes `r == closed_variant.rootn(5)` true for different operands, hiding the
openness a user set on purpose (a domain end). (4) `~r` would exclude `1/10**6` from the complement.

**when B is better**: if the owner reads "flags conservative" as "a to-nearest result should never be open at a
rounded piece's ends", valuing closed, hull-like results over faithful flags. no identified user story needs it.

### C. round the exact end too: one type per piece

two sub-variants that differ a lot in blast radius.

**C1, only when crossed** (the squeeze extension: result `[1e-06]`). meaning: if `lo > hi`, the two images round to
the same double (section 1), so return that double as a closed point, exactly as two float ends squeezing do.
pros: (1) the result is literally "the set of nearest doubles of the image", the purest statement of the class;
(2) one less odd-looking sliver; (3) `rootn(x, 5) ** 5` then gives a float point, which users of the nearest class
expect. cons: (1) **it drops an attained exact value in the closed variant**: `[1/10**30, 1.0000000000000003e-30]`
`.rootn(5)` would be `[1e-06]`, which does not contain `1/10**6 = rootn(10**-30, 5)`, a point the operand attains;
`test_every_value_is_in_the_result`'s `_holds(nearest)` on exact samples would be red at that end, so the current
contract has to be weakened to "an exact end survives unless a float end happens to round past it"; (2) the exact
end's type now depends on a rounding accident of its neighbour: `(10**-30, 1.0000000000000003e-30]` gives a float
point but `(10**-30, 1.0000000000000006e-30]` (two doubles up, image above the midpoint; inferred) keeps the exact end;
(3) still inconsistent with the applicator unless `evaluate_box` learns the same. when better: if the owner decides
that for the nearest class "a mixed piece's exact end is a courtesy, the float end is the contract" and prefers a
point over a sliver. low cost either way; the pins would move.

**C2, always** (per-operand float kind, as pow/hypot/fma/mod/steps/numeric already do: `functions.py:655`, `:746`,
`ops.py:489`, `:579`, `modulo.py:120`, `:328`, `reverse.py:655`, `:728`, `numeric.py`). meaning: in `_Function.end`,
`reverse._end` and the applicator corners, any finite float end in the piece (or operand) makes every end a float
rounded to nearest. the crossing class disappears (nearest rounding is monotone; two floats only squeeze).
pros: (1) one convention for the whole library instead of two (section 2 lists the split); (2) numpy-like promotion
that users of python floats find unsurprising; (3) `M(0, 1.0).sqrt()` = `[0.0, 1.0]` would match `M(0, 1.0) ** M(2)` =
`[0.0, 1.0]` today, which already differs from `M(0, 1.0) ** 2` = `[0, 1.0]` (probe 3), an inconsistency a user can
meet in two lines. cons: (1) a broad semantic change touching every op on every mixed piece: `M(0, 1.0) + 1/3` goes
from `[1/3, 1.3333333333333333]` to `[0.3333333333333333, 1.3333333333333333]`; `M(0, 1.0).exp()` from `[1, 2.718...]`
to `[1.0, 2.718...]` (probe 3 for today's values); the gate has oracles (`check_rounding_hook`, `exact_twins`, the
fuzz-symmetry decision that `-` keeps each cut's type, D26's per-end reading in the reverse ops) built on per-end
typing: weeks of pinned decisions reopen. (2) it does NOT buy isotonicity across types (plan §2 "fuzz-steps-isotone":
"no rule by number type can make it" when B is all-exact and A mixed), so the Q19 motive is absent. (3) exact ends of
mixed pieces lose their exactness in the nearest class, where an exact `0` or domain end next to a float is the
common case (`M(0, 0.1)`): `M(0, 1.0) * 10**400` would become a float piece ending in `inf` instead of `[0, 10**400]`
with an exact end past the doubles (inferred from the rule, not probed). (4) the applicator's "exact and infinite
operands never go through the hook" is one of its documented invariants (`applicator.py` module docstring).
when better: only if the owner wants to resolve the per-end/per-operand split library-wide before 2.0 AND picks
per-operand as the winner; then Q20 falls out. it is a decision about the whole class, not about this corner.

### D. raise (the behaviour before `912558b`)

a sound op on a legal operand must not raise; the fuzz called it a bug and the gate pins it red. listed for
completeness, rejected.

### E. directed fallback: when nearest crosses, round the float end away from the exact end (`(1/1000000, 1.0000000000000002e-06]`)

meaning: the float end borrows the outward class's direction only on a crossing. pros: the result holds every true
image value and keeps the exact end exactly. cons: it breaks the nearest promise at that end (the nearest double
`1e-06` is not in the closure, so `_nearest_holds` goes red), is not python's float, and makes the direction of
rounding depend on the neighbour. a user who wants true values has `OutwardMultiInterval`. when better: never in
this class; it is the outward class leaking in.

### F. the hull of both readings (`[1e-06, 1.0000000000000002e-06)`)

meaning: on a crossing, cover both the nearest double and the true image (nearest double, closed, up to the
outward-rounded end, open). pros: satisfies both oracles and holds the true values. cons: a two-ulp piece from a
sub-ulp input, a third rounding rule for one corner case, and the exact end's value is swallowed (not an end any
more). when better: never worth its own rule.

### G. empty with a warning

the image is non-empty; the soundness fuzz would be red at every sample. rejected.

## 5. weighing against Q19 and the outward class

**Q19 and Q20 are the same tension seen twice**: per-end typing means an exact end and a float end in one piece obey
different rules, and the fuzz found two consequences: non-isotonicity across types (Q19, outward) and a crossing
(Q20, nearest). the tempting joint answer is "one type per piece everywhere" (Q19's alternative = Q20's C2). two facts
argue against taking it for these reasons: (1) plan §2 "fuzz-steps-isotone" shows per-operand typing does not restore
isotonicity when the superset is all-exact, so Q19's alternative buys less than it says; (2) C2 does not make any
result more correct, it only moves exact ends onto doubles. so the joint answer should be the consistent one: keep
per-end typing in both (Q19 keep, Q20 A), document the two consequences next to each other in `v2-plan.md`
"flags at rounded ends" ("to nearest an exact end may sit on the far side of its float partner's nearest double; the
piece is the hull of the two") and move on. if the owner instead answers Q19 "make it isotone" by rounding exact
results in mixed operands, then C2 is the matching Q20 answer and should be done as one change with one review.

**the outward class does NOT have one type per piece**: Q19's own example `OutwardMultiInterval(1.0, 2) + 1/3` =
`(1.3333333333333333, 7/3]` is mixed, and `_Function.end` with `outward=True` keeps an exact end exact. what the
outward class has that the nearest class lacks is ORDER-PRESERVING rounding: `DOWN` and `UP` move an end away from
the other, so the exact and float ends can never swap (plan §2 "fuzz-rootn-crossed": "outward rounding never
crosses"; probe 3's outward rows). so "as the outward class's open ends are" in the Q20 bullet is best read as
"outward's ends are already one consistent object per end: an exact value or a directed double, open if moved";
A gives the nearest class the same property per end (an exact value or the nearest double, flag kept). the
applicator's `min`/`max` of corner values is what both classes already do for `+ - * /`; A extends it to the
functions and the reverse ops, which is the consistent completion.

**D26 precedent**: the nearest class already keeps a double the operand's `x` excludes when a part rounds wholly onto
it (`reverse._keep_squeezed`; README row "reverse ops and `x`, to nearest"). a piece holding a double outside the true
image is therefore not new in this class; it is what rounding to nearest means here.

## 6. recommendation, confidence, what would change my mind, cost of changing later

**recommendation: keep A (as built), and write it down.** confidence: **high (0.85)** that A is the right rule under
the library's stated contract; **medium (0.6)** that the owner will find the sliver acceptable on sight, which is why
the documentation matters more than the code.

what to write (not done here, read-only): (1) `v2-plan.md` "current design" > arithmetic > "flags at rounded ends", one
sentence: to nearest, a float end's nearest double may lie past an exact end of the same piece; the piece is then the
hull of the two values, each keeping its flag, the rule `evaluate_box` has always used for `+ - * /` (with the
`*` example from probe 2 as the demonstration that it predates `912558b`); (2) the `_settled` docstring already says it;
(3) a decision-log line answering Q20. optional, cheap: a pin that the applicator's `*` sliver and `_settled`'s agree
(`M(1 - 10**-30, 1.0) * 1/3` beside `rootn`'s), so the two producers cannot drift apart again.

**what would change my mind**:
* the owner wants a crossed piece to be a point (`[1e-06]`): then C1, accepting that the closed variant drops an
  attained exact value and `_holds(nearest)` for exact samples must be weakened; I would then prefer C1 over B.
* the owner decides Q19 by moving to per-operand typing: then C2 here, as one library-wide change with the applicator
  (and the fuzz-symmetry, D26 and `exact_twins` oracles revisited), not as a Q20 patch.
* a user story where an open exact end next to a float must be read as closed (B): I found none; the applicator does
  not do it either.
* a second crossing site outside `_settled` and `branch_preimage` (`_angle_box`, `_power_corner`, `hypot`, the steps):
  the plan's probe swept functions and bases but not atan2/pow/hypot pairs at sub-ulp mixed ends. `pow_` and `hypot`
  are per-operand (no exact end to cross); `atan2` types per box (`_angle_box`'s `as_float`), so also none; the
  applicator orders by value. inferred, not probed: a sweep would close it.

**cost of changing later**:
* A -> B or C1 **before 2.0**: an afternoon: two three-line edits (`_settled`, `branch_preimage`), two pins moved, the
  `@example` re-run, a gate; the fuzz oracle `_holds(nearest)` needs an exemption for C1. **after 2.0**: a result
  change on a documented rule; low user impact (sub-ulp mixed pieces), but it is the kind of silent set change that
  becomes expensive after release, and the applicator would then disagree with the functions unless it changes too.
* A -> C2 before 2.0: a week-scale semantic change across applicator, functions, reverse, with the per-end oracles and
  decisions (fuzz-symmetry, D26, fuzz-mixed-points) rewritten; after 2.0: effectively a 3.0 of the nearest class.
* staying on A costs one paragraph of documentation now and nothing later.
